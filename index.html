Step 1: Confirm Which Option Fits Your Evening

Dine-in, pickup, or delivery

The restaurant offers dine-in, takeout with pickup, and delivery, so your first decision is how you want to get the food. Each option has a different timeline and a different level of control over how the food arrives.

Delivery is the easiest choice if you are staying home, but it depends on the restaurant being open and taking orders for your address at that time. Pickup is often the better pick if you live close by and want your food hot, since you can take it straight from the counter. Dine-in works well for a relaxed meal with friends.

Step 2: Build Your Order Around the Menu

Know the main sections

The menu is organized by type of dish, which makes planning easier. Sections include Appetizers, Soups, Grill Tandoor Special, Vegetable Special, Bread, Biryani Rice Special, Lamb Special, Seafood Special, Thali Special, Chicken Special, Sauce and Pickle, Desserts, and Drinks. Looking at the sections first helps you choose a balanced order rather than picking items one at a time.

Think about how each dish travels

Not every dish arrives in the same condition. Curries and dals usually hold their texture and warmth well in a sealed container. Fried items such as pakora or samosas can lose their crispness during transport, so you may want to order them when you plan to eat quickly or pick up in person.

Biryani is another dish worth discussing. Ask the staff how they package rice dishes so you know what to expect when you open the container.

Match the order to your group

A good order usually includes a mix of textures and flavors. For a small group, you might choose one grilled dish such as chicken tikka, one curry such as butter chicken or chana masala, and a bread like garlic naan. For a larger table, add a biryani, a vegetable dish like saag paneer or dal makhani, and a dessert such as ras malai or gajar halwa.

Step 3: Ask About Dietary Needs and Spice Levels

Vegetarian and vegan questions

The menu includes several vegetarian choices, including veg samosa, paneer tikka, kadai paneer, malai kofta, and aloo gobi. If someone in your group avoids meat, ask the staff which dishes are prepared without meat and whether any sauces contain animal products. Be specific about your needs rather than assuming a dish is safe.

Halal and special requests

The official website lists halal food, which many diners look for. If halal preparation matters to you, ask the restaurant to confirm which items you plan to order. Staff can give you the most accurate answer for each dish.

Spice levels

Indian and Nepali cooking can range from mild to very hot. Momos come in steamed, fried, sadeko, and spicy versions, so the same dish can feel very different depending on preparation. Tell the staff how much heat you prefer, and ask whether a dish can be adjusted before you commit.

Step 4: Ask About Timing and Packaging

Ask how long your order will take

Preparation times depend on how busy the kitchen is. When you call, ask how long the order is likely to take and whether a large order will need extra time. Planning around that estimate keeps your food from sitting too long.

Ask how items are packed

Sauces, chutneys, and bread should stay separate from wet dishes so they keep their texture. Ask whether the restaurant packs items separately and whether utensils are included. If you ordered several curries, ask whether they come in individual containers so the group can divide the meal easily.

Reheat smartly at home

Once you get home, transfer curries into a pan on low heat and add a splash of water if the sauce has thickened. Warm naan briefly in a dry pan or wrap it in foil in the oven. These small steps help leftover-style reheating taste closer to fresh.
